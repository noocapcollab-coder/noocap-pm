# PM lessons: Briefs & scripts

Mistakes the PM made once and the rule that came out of each.

## A script the brand already has never goes again unless it was revised
When: A card sits at 5- Script Approval and its script already went to the brand from this or another deal, with no new brand notes since
Do: Leave it: no Script draft. If one gets drafted and the send guard says the scripts already went, retire it at once
Never: Never draft the same script a second time, and never leave such a duplicate waiting in Approvals as Needs you
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## Another campaign's brief shared for an overview is not the brief
When: A brand shares a brief link 'in the meantime' or 'for an overview' (often an older or current campaign's brief) and says the final brief for our deal will come later
Do: Treat it as reference: thank them if a reply is due, keep the card at 2- Waiting For Brief with the brand's posting window as POST DATE, and wait for the final brief before the script writer starts.
Never: File it as the deal's brief, move the card to Script Draft, ask Rudraksh for a script or send a script written from it.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## A new script at 5 goes to the brand whatever the deal stage says
When: A video card is back at 5- Script Approval with a script the brand hasn't had, while the deal stage still shows a later step (Submitted, Approved) from an earlier round
Do: Send the card's newest script (the part under UPDATED / REVISED SCRIPT) as the PDF, and put the team's NOTES from the card into the email word for word. Only the newest version goes in the PDF.
Never: Never hold a script because the deal stage is stale, never put the old script or our internal notes in the PDF, and never promise the brand 'the revised script shortly' without it going out.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A brand that hands over a brief and asks for the script has sent a brief
When: Reading a brand email that attaches or links a brief and asks when the script will come, above all on a deal that is already posted or paid (the next reel's brief)
Do: Read it as a brief, so the next reel gets its own deal and card and the script writer gets the brief
Never: Never file it as a plain brand_reply on the old deal. A brief only promised for later, or a rate question, is not a brief.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## A brand's own rewritten script is their revised script, not notes for the writer
When: The brand sends back its OWN rewritten version of our script (in the email or attached: "we've made a few adjustments to the script, revised version below", "we've attached our revised version, does it work for the creator?") without saying it is approved
Do: Treat their version as the revised script. Keep the card where it is, save their text on the card, and ask Harsh once on WhatsApp whether the creator films it as written; his answer becomes the reply. If they also say it is approved, it is an approval (card to To Film).
Never: Send it to Rudraksh as script changes, move the card to 4.8, or reply "we've passed your notes to our writer, revised script in 2 to 3 working days".
Seen 2 time(s). Source: System check.
## A cold 'share your usual rates' email is an inquiry, never a brief
When: A brand writes for the first time asking if the creator is interested and what their rates are, with a description of the idea
Do: Open the deal at Inbound and put it to Harsh for a rate; no card, no script
Never: Never file it as a brief, open a deal at Brief Received or ask Rudraksh for a script before a price is agreed
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A repeat deal whose card belongs to the old deal still sends its script
When: A brand comes back for a new campaign in the same thread, a new deal opens, and the script is written on the existing card that is still linked to the old deal
Do: Use the deal's one matching card when it has no Linked Video, and link the new deal to it, but only when that card's script has not already gone to the brand in a Script email from any deal
Never: Never let a script sit at 5 because the card is linked to an older, invoiced deal of the same brand, and never re-send a script that already went from the other deal of the same campaign
Seen 2 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A revised script must really differ from the one the brand already has
When: A script card comes back to 5- Script Approval after the brand's script notes
Do: Send it as the revised script only when the card's script text changed since the last Script email (its fingerprint differs); otherwise leave it with Rudraksh
Never: Never send the same script again saying the brand's notes are worked in, never send the same revised script twice, and never send another 'revised' script when the brand has given no new notes since the last one we sent
Seen 2 time(s). Source: System check.
## Notes on our script are script changes, even when the brand calls it a video draft
When: A brand sends notes while the deal is at Script Sent (or earlier) and no cut has gone out, but the email says 'your first video draft' or 'the video'
Do: Check what we actually sent: only a script means script_changes, the notes go to Rudraksh on the card and the revised script goes back when the card returns to 5
Never: Never file it as video_feedback or hand it to Harsh as a reader split when no cut exists
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## One email about several scripts: move only the card the brand approved
When: A deal has several script cards and the brand's email approves one script and rejects or comments on another
Do: Match the approval to the card it names ("the second script" = card (2)) and move only that card to 6- To Film. Send the other script's notes to the script writer on its own card, back at 4- Script Draft.
Never: Move the first script card found, or save the brand's rejection of one script as another card's approved script.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## A deal paid upfront still sends its revised script
When: A brand that paid 100% before the video sends script notes, and the card comes back to 5- Script Approval with the updated script
Do: Send the revised script to the brand in its thread as for any live deal, with only the newest version in the PDF; a Paid stage means the money is in, not that the work is done
Never: Never treat a Paid deal with a Script Sent Date as finished while its video is not posted, and never send the old and the updated script together in one PDF
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## Brand script feedback moves the card to 4.8- Script Changes; back at 5 the revised script goes
When: A brand sends changes on a script we already sent
Do: Move every card of that deal still in scripting to 4.8- Script Changes, put the brand's notes on the card, and tag Rudraksh. When the card is moved back to 5, send the script again as the revised version, all cards ready together in one email
Never: Never leave the card at 5 or 4.5 after brand feedback (nobody can tell it needs work), and never treat a card returning to 5 after changes as already sent
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A card moved to 5 sends its script, including later videos of a posted deal and revised scripts
When: A video card reaches 5- Script Approval (from 4.5 Internal Review or any earlier status)
Do: Send the script unless that exact version already went. Every Script email records a fingerprint of each script it carried, and a card at 5 whose script differs from the sent version goes again as a revised script
Never: Never let the deal's stage (Posted after video 1 went live) block the scripts of its other videos, and never re-send a script whose content hasn't changed
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A deal paid upfront still sends its script when the card reaches Script Approval
When: A deal is already Paid or Invoiced (upfront payment) and its video card moves to 5- Script Approval
Do: Send the script email the same way as any other deal, the same day
Never: Never skip or delay the script because the deal stage is already Paid or Invoiced
Seen 2 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A brief is not a contract, even when the email mentions the agreement
When: A brand email carries a PDF and talks about a contract or agreement
Do: Treat it as a contract only when the file is named or written as one, or the brand says it is the contract to sign. If the email calls the file a brief or says the agreement comes later ("once you confirm, I will share the formal agreement"), it is a brief: file it on the deal's card and answer what the brand asked (usually a date for Harsh).
Never: Send "thanks for sending the contract, we'll review and sign it" or a Contract alert for a brief.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## The brand's brief file always lands on the card and reaches the script writer
When: A brand sends the brief as a PDF, Word or slides file, in any email of the thread
Do: Attach the file on the video card inside the brief and tell the script writer it's there; if the email with the file was held or too big, fetch it from Gmail
Never: Never write a 'Source email' section (sender, subject, date) on a card, and never leave the brief file only in Gmail
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## No money for the team, but the brief's own content words still reach the script writer
When: A brand note uses a money word about the video itself (budget pressure, Black Friday price on the end card, it cost me a coffee, paid partnership label)
Do: Send that line to the script writer. Anything the money filter does hold back goes to Harsh in #pm so nothing disappears silently
Never: Drop a content note because it contains the word budget, price or cost
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A deal past the brief always has a video card
When: A deal reaches Brief Received or later, including deals opened by the system check or by hand
Do: Make the card with the brief, link it in the deal's Linked Video and tell the script writer once. When the creator's board already has the brand's card, link that card instead of making a new one
Never: Leave a deal with a brief and no card, so nobody is writing the script
Seen 3 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## Script notes go to the card still being scripted, never to a filmed video
When: A deal has several videos and the brand sends script notes or a script approval
Do: Put the notes on the sibling cards at status 5 or lower (CapCut x Chris (2), (3)) and ping the script writer with those cards
Never: Move a card that is filmed, with the brand or approved back to 4 or 6, or put the next script's notes on it
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## A brand describing its review process is not an approval
When: A brand email mentions script review, approval or filming as steps still to come, before any script was sent
Do: Count a script as approved only when the brand approves a script we actually sent (Script Sent Date or a sent Script email). 'Upon our approval', 'send us the script for review' and a step-by-step guide are instructions: answer what they ask for now.
Never: Set Script Approved, move a card to 6 or draft a tool-access email when no script has been sent.
Seen 3 time(s). Source: System check.
## Include the script link in the draft email
When: Sending a script to the brand for review
Do: Add the Google Drive or Frame.io link to the script in the email body so the brand can access it immediately.
Never: Send a script email without the link to the actual file
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Draft edit.
## A script the creator's team sends by hand is a sent script
When: Our side (the creator or their team) emails the script into the brand's thread before the PM's own Script email goes out
Do: Record it like the PM's own send: Script Sent Date and Last Our Reply from that email, Next Action 'waiting on the brand to approve', and retire the PM's waiting Script draft.
Never: Leave the deal saying 'send the script' or with an empty Script Sent Date, because follow-ups and the 'script at 5 with no email' check then work from wrong facts.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: System check.
## No team ping for work that hasn't started
When: A brand asks to pause, or any update arrives, on a deal with no brief and no video card yet
Do: Keep the hold on the deal and keep following up with the brand, but only tell the script writer or an editor when a video card exists for that deal, meaning work was actually handed to them.
Never: Never ping Rudraksh or an editor to stop or change work on a video they were never given.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Harsh correction.
## Scripts at Script Approval go to the brand
When: A card reaches 5- Script Approval
Do: Share the script with the brand in the deal's existing thread (WhatsApp deals: tell Harsh). When a sponsor card reaches 5 and no deal links to it, find the brand's open deal and link the card to it instead of only alerting "script ready, no deal".
Never: Leave it unsent or send it in a new thread.
Seen 2 time(s). Source: Rulebook.
## An approval with the brand's own script is an approval
When: Brand/platform says 'Approved Idea, Script', 'validated version', or Next Step 'Draft video'
Do: Card to To Film, deal Script Approved, save their version as the final script.
Never: Send it to Rudraksh as changes or a brief update.
Seen 1 time(s). Source: Rulebook.
## No money for the team
When: Writing cards, briefs, team pings, the access page
Do: Keep only creative content.
Never: Mention a rate, price, fee, budget, $ or payment.
Seen 3 time(s). Source: Rulebook.
## One video card per reel
When: A deal covers 2+ reels, or a brief arrives for the next reel
Do: Make a card per reel; put the next reel's brief on its own card.
Never: Put reel 2's brief on reel 1's finished card.
Seen 4 time(s). Source: Rulebook.
